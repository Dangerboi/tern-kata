# Triggers follow kata's contract; catch-up is computed from a local checkpoint

The worker schedules enabled adapter jobs: `execute` jobs and `notify` jobs on existing Issues. It never schedules native recurring-child jobs (`notify` with a per-run Issue policy). For each job it keeps a checkpoint of what it has handled and applies `overlap`, `catchup`, `grace_seconds` and `timeout_seconds` when it admits work.

herdr-kata (definitions.md "Triggers and catch-up", issue-schedules.md, kata-adapter.md "Ownership and dispatch") and kata (cron.md "Adapter responsibilities") are the sources. Where they differ, kata's contract wins, because definitions are shared and a job must mean the same to every client.

## Decisions

- **Cron syntax: adopt.** kata v0.20.0 parses with `github.com/netresearch/go-cron` v0.16.1 using `Minute | Hour | Dom | Month | Dow | Descriptor | DowOrDom` (`internal/cron/triggers.go`), and the Pane's schedule words, previews and the worker use exactly that parser at that version. `DowOrDom` keeps crontab's rule that when both day fields are restricted, a day matching either one runs. Sunday as `7`, wrap-around ranges and weekday steps are accepted; a step as large as its range (`*/60`, `0 */24 * * *`) and `@every 0s` are refused, and a time skipped by a spring-forward change runs later that day. The worker honours the trigger's `timezone` with its daylight-saving behaviour; an embedded `TZ=` or `CRON_TZ=` directive is read only when the trigger's `timezone` is empty, or names the same zone. A directive naming another zone than a non-empty `timezone` sets the job aside, never running it in either zone, with the reason "its cron expression names zone X but the trigger's timezone is Y", shown by `worker status`, `worker doctor` and the Job card like any other job problem. kata's contract says portable cron triggers use the configured trigger timezone, and its native recurring jobs already require the two to agree; refusing avoids running at a time neither author meant. A directive without a schedule is refused. What the library does is what runs; the Pane words only the common shapes and reads any other as "cron <expression>".
- **Trigger kinds: adopt, one change.** `manual` fires only on request. `cron`, `interval` (anchored at the job's `created_at`) and `once` (`at`) as in herdr-kata, with `@every` accepted for adapter jobs and anchored the same way. `issue-scheduled` is supported (herdr-kata rejects it, pointing users at explicit requests, a path ADR-0002 does not adopt): the worker reads the Issue's planning-date instant with `GET .../issues/{ref}/planning-dates` once per Issue revision, keeps a timer for it, and re-reads on Issue events (ADR-0004). `issue-deadline` is allowed for `notify` only; kata refuses it for `execute`, and deadline never gates readiness. Kata also requires an executing job with an Issue-date trigger to report to the Issue that trigger fires on (`issue target differs from date source`), so the Job card keeps one Issue for it; only a notify job may name a second Issue (the one it notifies about) beside its trigger's, and the card models, loads and resolves that one separately.
- **Notify jobs: adopt kata's rule.** The worker sends the inbox notification for adapter `notify` jobs on existing Issues. A zero-lead job with the `current-owner-or-author` recipient and an Issue-date trigger that notifies the trigger's own Issue (its `issue` omitted or naming the trigger's `issue_uid`) is the one kata's own date sweeper already notifies, so the worker skips it; custom leads and recipients, and a job that notifies a different Issue than its trigger's, are the worker's. Recipients resolve from a fresh Issue read.
- **Checkpoint: adopt.** Handled-through and pending occurrences are saved atomically per job (ADR-0006). A new worker starts at the later of its first start and the job's creation. A definition change supersedes unadmitted pending work. Occurrences accumulated during a route outage use the same catch-up policy when access returns.
- **Catch-up: adopt.** `skip`: only the newest missed occurrence within `grace_seconds` (zero or absent grace admits nothing missed). `latest`: the newest. `all`: every missed one, in order. Bounded to 7 days and 100 pending per job; overflow drops the oldest and logs it. Missed occurrences while the Project was refused follow the same rules; a disabled job schedules nothing: when it is enabled again it starts from that moment and never runs the time it was off, with or without a worker restart in between. Occurrences already waiting (parked or pending) when it was disabled are dropped when it is enabled again: kata gives every job write, enabling included, a new definition event, and a changed definition supersedes waiting work like any edit. A job disabled and enabled again entirely while the worker is down is not noticed as disabled, so its time off is still caught up. A job disabled while the worker is up and enabled while it is down resumes when the worker first sees it enabled, not at the enable.
- **Refresh before admission: adopt.** Admission refreshes the job definition and refuses to start scheduled work from a cached copy; the cache serves labelled reads and `worker status` only (kata cron.md). The one exception is a burst of Issue events: a tick that answers an Issue event may reuse a listing younger than two seconds, so the burst lists the jobs once; a job event since that listing forces a fresh one. An occurrence that comes due, or admission after a run ended, always lists again. Runs already prepared keep their frozen definitions.
- **High-rate warning: adopt.** A per-run job that creates more than 24 Issues a day is shown with "creates about N Issues a day; consider an existing Issue" on the Jobs page and in `worker doctor`; it never stops the job.
- **Set aside individually: adopt.** An invalid or unsupported definition does not stop other jobs. It is listed with its reason on the Jobs page and in `worker status`: an `on_fail` or `on_failure` workflow (ADR-0008), an unmapped checkout, profile or secret, a timeout over `max_timeout`, a prompt over its cap, `issue-deadline` on `execute`.
- **Run now: adopt.** `R` and `tern-kata run` ask the worker over the control socket for a manual occurrence of any adapter job whatever its trigger or author, with the usual refresh, readiness (open Issue, not `someday`, not scheduled for later, for an `execute` job; a parked Issue refuses the request while a scheduled occurrence waits for it without a slot, with a timed wake at the Issue's `scheduled_on` instant), overlap and capacity rules. Refused for native jobs and when no worker serves the Project.
- **Issue date offsets: adopt both.** `scheduled_offset_seconds` and `deadline_offset_seconds` are kata's per-run Issue date offsets; the worker sets both on the Issue it creates (spec story 71). herdr-kata supports only the deadline one and marks the other unsupported; there is no reason here to refuse a field kata defines.

## Consequences

- `issue-scheduled` execute jobs wake within a second of the date through the timer plus the 1-minute recompute; herdr-kata's ten-minute delay for the same case (a date maturing alone wakes no one) does not apply.
- A job whose cron expression kata's parser and a later parser read differently runs as kata's does; the Job card's preview and the worker agree because both use one parser.
- The parser is not the only shared rule. The Cron job module (`internal/cronjob`) is where the rules of a Cron job live: the typed kinds, which jobs are Native jobs, the schedule of a job with the anchor rule written once (an adapter job's interval and `@every` count from its creation, a native job's interval from the delivery anchor on its parent, a native job's `@every` stays unanchored as kata's trigger documentation has it), Issues a day and the high-rate warning, and the prefix and reader of a native job's delivery state. The Pane and the worker both read a job through it, and kata's generated client types are translated into it in one place (`internal/cronjobclient`), so a change to one of these rules is made once. "One parser" still stands as the decision.
